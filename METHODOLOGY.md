# How the review works

Paper Review is not "an AI's opinion of your draft." It is a structured method
that runs your draft through six phases and five review lenses, applies the
**recognized reporting standard that matches your paper type**, and returns a
verdict calibrated to how finished the draft is. The rigor comes from
**established, citable checklists** — not criteria we invented. This page explains
the whole method in plain language.

---

## The six-phase method

One model plays every role below in sequence, within a single pass. (It is not a
real multi-agent system — see [LIMITATIONS.md](LIMITATIONS.md). The "panel" framing
is a way to force genuinely separate, self-challenging perspectives.)

1. **Intake** — triage the draft (field, thesis, sections, gaps) and, critically,
   **classify the paper type** so the right methodological standard gets applied.
2. **Specialist panel (×5)** — five reviewers each critique from **one lens only**,
   score it 0–10, and anchor every issue to a quote from the draft. The Rigor lens
   loads the type-matched checklist as mandatory criteria.
3. **Rebuttal round** — each lens reads the others and concurs, dissents, or
   nuances. This is where plausible-but-wrong issues get knocked down and real ones
   get sharpened — the cross-examination, not the first take, is the product.
4. **Moderator** — merges both rounds, dedupes, groups related
   reporting-standard gaps into one issue, surfaces contradictions and any
   transparency/ethics flag, and ranks the top issues.
5. **Chief editor** — writes the human-readable referee letter (Verdict /
   Strengths / Issues to Fix / Optional Improvements), length-scaled to the draft.
6. **Scorecard** — the five dimension scores, the overall mean, and the verdict,
   applied by the literal rubric for your draft stage.

You receive the **letter** and the **scorecard**. The internal panel detail is
available on request.

---

## The five lenses

These map one-to-one to the five scorecard columns. They are not an ad-hoc list —
they mirror the criteria journals and funders actually score on (empirical
taxonomy of referee criteria: Bornmann, Nast & Daniel 2008; convergent
Nature/Science/PLOS reviewer guidelines; NIH review criteria).

| Lens | Column | What it asks |
|---|---|---|
| **Significance & Originality** | Significance | What is genuinely new here, and does it matter? Is the contribution positioned against prior work? (Scores the contribution the draft demonstrates, not the importance of the topic.) |
| **Rigor & Validity** | Rigor | Is the method sound and the inference valid? **Applies the type-matched reporting standard.** For non-empirical work, checks logical soundness and scope instead. |
| **Evidence & Grounding** | Evidence | Are claims grounded in cited work or data? Is the evidence sufficient for the strength of the claim, or is there overreach? |
| **Argument & Structure** | Argument | Is the thesis explicit? Do the reasons support it (Toulmin: claim → grounds → warrant)? Are counter-arguments handled? Is the scaffold coherent? |
| **Clarity & Style** | Clarity | Prose: grammar, register, sentence logic, paragraph rhythm, undefined jargon. (Weighted lightly at sketch/student stages.) |

The Rigor lens is the one that adapts most. Empirical work lives or dies on it,
which is why it carries the reporting standard below.

## Paper type → reporting standard

This is the heart of the method. **The criteria adapt to what you actually wrote.**
A trial, an observational study, a systematic review, a qualitative study, a
theory paper, and an essay are each appraised against a *different* recognized
standard — not one generic checklist. The intake editor classifies the type; the
Rigor lens loads the matching standard and names it in the review so the appraisal
is traceable.

| Paper type | Standard applied | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Randomized / clinical trial | **CONSORT** — randomization, allocation concealment, blinding, pre-specified primary outcome, participant flow, effect size + CI | [EQUATOR · CONSORT](https://www.equator-network.org/reporting-guidelines/consort/) |
| Observational study (cohort / case-control / cross-sectional) | **STROBE** — design stated, participant selection, confounders adjusted, bias sources, generalizability, no causal language from associational data | [EQUATOR · STROBE](https://www.equator-network.org/reporting-guidelines/strobe/) |
| Systematic review / meta-analysis | **PRISMA** (+ **AMSTAR-2**) — research question (PICO), reproducible search, inclusion criteria, risk-of-bias assessment, synthesis, publication-bias check | [EQUATOR · PRISMA](https://www.equator-network.org/reporting-guidelines/prisma/) |
| Qualitative study | **COREQ** (/ SRQR) — sampling rationale & saturation, reflexivity/positionality, data-collection method, analysis approach, trustworthiness | [EQUATOR · COREQ](https://www.equator-network.org/reporting-guidelines/coreq/) |
| Argumentative essay / theory paper | **Toulmin** model — claim · grounds · warrant · backing · qualifier · rebuttal; coherence and scope. (No methods checklist.) | Toulmin, *The Uses of Argument* (1958) |
| Research proposal / grant | **NIH** criteria — Significance · Innovation · Approach · Feasibility | [NIH peer-review criteria](https://grants.nih.gov/grants/peer/peer.htm) |

Other types are handled too — prediction/model studies (**TRIPOD/STARD**), animal
studies (**ARRIVE**), generic quantitative work (the four-validity typology of
Shadish, Cook & Campbell 2002), and literature reviews (coverage + synthesis
quality). All of these reporting guidelines (CONSORT, STROBE, PRISMA, COREQ,
ARRIVE, TRIPOD/STARD) are cataloged by the
[EQUATOR Network](https://www.equator-network.org/), the recognized library of
health-research reporting standards. Only the **one** matching standard is applied
to a given draft — never all of them at once.

## Stage calibration

A class essay should not be judged like a journal submission. So before scoring,
the method sets a tone and a verdict threshold from the **draft stage** you state
(or that it infers from your wording):

- **final** — submission-ready; full publication-grade scrutiny, no softening.
- **working** — in progress; focus on issues that would meaningfully improve the
  next revision, skip nitpicks. *(This is the default if you don't say.)*
- **sketch** — early outline; judge whether the core idea and structure are on
  track; skip language polish.
- **student** — coursework; the voice of a supportive writing tutor; reporting
  standards named as things to learn, not blockers.

**Stage is the #1 thing that changes the verdict.** Tell the skill your stage —
"working draft," "final submission," "rough sketch," "student essay" — so it grades
you fairly.

## The score and the verdict

Each lens scores 0–10, calibrated so a **competent draft scores 7–8** (9 is rare,
10 essentially never; below 5 means genuinely broken, not "has minor issues").
The **Overall** is the arithmetic mean of the five, to one decimal.

The verdict applies a **literal threshold table keyed to your draft stage** — the
same overall score yields a kinder verdict for a student essay than for a final
submission:

| Stage | Accept ≥ | Minor Revision ≥ | Major Revision ≥ | below → Reject |
|---|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| final | 8.5 | 7.0 | 5.0 | < 5.0 |
| working | 7.5 | 6.0 | 4.0 | < 4.0 |
| sketch | 6.5 | 5.0 | 3.0 | < 3.0 |
| student | 7.0 | 5.5 | 3.5 | < 3.5 |

Two refinements keep it honest:

- **Accept tolerates one high-priority issue.** A single sharp, fixable blocker is
  the normal state of a strong working draft, so it doesn't force a downgrade —
  but a second blocker does.
- **Boundary tie-break.** When the mean lands within 0.2 of a threshold, the count
  of genuine unresolved blockers — not rounding — decides which side it falls on,
  and the verdict prose says so.

## Why this is trustworthy

The criteria are not invented for this tool. They are **established, published,
citable standards** — STROBE, CONSORT, PRISMA, COREQ, the Toulmin model, NIH
review criteria — that journals, funders, and the EQUATOR Network already use. The
review names the standard it applied so you can look it up and check the work.
That is the difference between a critique and a compliment. For the honest
boundaries of the method, see [LIMITATIONS.md](LIMITATIONS.md).
